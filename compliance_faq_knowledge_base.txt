FAQ ID: GST-001
Topic: GST Fundamentals
Question: What is GST and how does the Indian indirect tax system operate?
Keywords: gst, goods and services tax, indirect tax, cgst, sgst, igst, dual gst, destination based tax, tax structure
Answer: Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a destination-based indirect tax levied on the manufacture, sale, and consumption of goods and services throughout India. It subsumed multiple central and state indirect taxes (including Excise Duty, Service Tax, VAT, and CST) into a unified structure under the motto "One Nation, One Tax". India operates a dual GST model where both the Centre and the States simultaneously levy tax on a common taxable base. Intra-state supplies attract Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST) or Union Territory GST (UTGST), split equally. Inter-state supplies attract Integrated GST (IGST), collected by the Central Government and apportioned to destination states.
Source Name: Goods and Services Tax Portal
Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/
Additional Source URL: https://cbic-gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-002
Topic: GST Registration
Question: What are the turnover thresholds and criteria for mandatory GST registration?
Keywords: gst registration, registration threshold, mandatory registration, 40 lakhs, 20 lakhs, casual taxable person, inter state supply, reverse charge, ecommerce
Answer: GST registration is mandatory for businesses whose aggregate annual turnover exceeds the statutory threshold limit. For suppliers dealing exclusively in goods, the threshold is Rs 40 Lakhs (Rs 20 Lakhs for Special Category States). For service providers and mixed suppliers, the aggregate turnover threshold is Rs 20 Lakhs (Rs 10 Lakhs for Special Category States). Registration is compulsory irrespective of turnover in specific cases: persons making inter-state taxable supplies, casual taxable persons, non-resident taxable persons, persons liable to pay tax under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM), e-commerce operators, and persons supplying online information database access or retrieval (OIDAR) services. Application is filed electronically via Form GST REG-01 on the GST portal.
Source Name: Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC)
Source URL: https://cbic-gst.gov.in/
Additional Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-003
Topic: GSTIN
Question: What is GSTIN and what is its 15-digit structure?
Keywords: gstin, gst identification number, 15 digit, state code, pan based, checksum, gst number
Answer: GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number) is a unique 15-character alphanumeric state-wise PAN-based identification number issued to every registered taxpayer. The structure is: the first 2 digits represent the Census State Code (e.g., '07' for Delhi, '27' for Maharashtra); digits 3 to 12 represent the 10-character Permanent Account Number (PAN) of the business entity or proprietor; the 13th digit indicates the entity code (the number of registrations the entity holds in that state, from 1-9 then A-Z); the 14th digit is the default character 'Z'; and the 15th digit is a checksum character used for error detection. The GSTIN must be displayed on business signboards and all tax invoices.
Source Name: Goods and Services Tax Portal
Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-004
Topic: GST Returns
Question: What are regular GST returns and what is the return filing structure?
Keywords: gst returns, regular returns, gstr filing, compliance calendar, monthly return, quarterly return, qrmp
Answer: Registered GST taxpayers must periodically file statutory returns detailing outward supplies, inward supplies, input tax credit availed, and net tax paid. Regular taxpayers file Form GSTR-1 (monthly or quarterly under the QRMP scheme) to report outward sales, and Form GSTR-3B (monthly summary return) to declare summary tax liabilities and claim Input Tax Credit (ITC). Small taxpayers with annual turnover up to Rs 5 Crore may opt for the Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) scheme. Annual compliance involves Form GSTR-9 (annual return) and GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement for prescribed turnover limits). Failure to file returns on time attracts late fees under Section 47 and mandatory interest on delayed tax payment under Section 50 of the CGST Act.
Source Name: Goods and Services Tax Portal
Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/
Additional Source URL: https://cbic-gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-005
Topic: GSTR-1
Question: What is GSTR-1 and what details must be reported in it?
Keywords: gstr-1, gstr1, outward supplies, sales return, b2b, b2c, invoice reporting, hsn summary, credit notes
Answer: GSTR-1 is a mandatory statement of outward supplies of goods and services made by a registered taxpayer during a tax period. It contains invoice-level details of Business-to-Business (B2B) supplies, large inter-state Business-to-Consumer (B2C) sales, summary values of small B2C sales, debit and credit notes issued, zero-rated exports, exempt supplies, and an HSN-wise summary of outward supplies. For monthly filers, GSTR-1 is due by the 11th of the following month; under the QRMP scheme, quarterly GSTR-1 or the optional Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF) is filed. Invoices uploaded in GSTR-1 auto-populate into the corresponding buyers' Form GSTR-2A and Form GSTR-2B for claiming Input Tax Credit.
Source Name: Goods and Services Tax Portal
Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-006
Topic: GSTR-3B
Question: What is GSTR-3B and how is tax liability discharged?
Keywords: gstr-3b, gstr3b, summary return, tax payment, electronic cash ledger, electronic credit ledger, tax discharge
Answer: GSTR-3B is a monthly self-assessed summary return in which registered regular taxpayers summarize their total taxable outward supplies, reverse charge inward supplies, eligible and ineligible Input Tax Credit (ITC) availed, and compute net tax liability. After setting off available ITC balances in the Electronic Credit Ledger in the legally prescribed order (IGST first against IGST, then CGST/SGST; followed by CGST/SGST credits), any remaining tax dues must be discharged by depositing cash into the Electronic Cash Ledger via challan (PMT-06). GSTR-3B is typically due by the 20th of the subsequent month for monthly filers (or 22nd/24th for QRMP quarterly filers). GSTR-3B cannot be revised once filed.
Source Name: Goods and Services Tax Portal
Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-007
Topic: Input Tax Credit
Question: What is Input Tax Credit (ITC) and what are the essential conditions to claim it?
Keywords: input tax credit, itc, gstr-2b, section 16, eligible itc, blocked credit, section 17(5), tax invoice, payment within 180 days
Answer: Input Tax Credit (ITC) allows a registered taxpayer to deduct the GST paid on business purchases and expenses from the output GST liability due on sales, thereby eliminating the cascading effect (tax on tax). Under Section 16 of the CGST Act, four key statutory conditions must be satisfied: 1) The buyer possesses a valid tax invoice or debit note issued by a registered supplier; 2) The goods or services have been received; 3) The supplier has reported the invoice in GSTR-1 and it appears in the buyer's auto-generated Form GSTR-2B; 4) The supplier has actually paid the tax to the government and filed GSTR-3B; and 5) The recipient pays the supplier the invoice value and tax within 180 days from the invoice date. Certain goods and services (e.g., motor vehicles for personal use, food and beverages, club memberships) are blocked from ITC under Section 17(5).
Source Name: Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC)
Source URL: https://cbic-gst.gov.in/
Additional Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-008
Topic: GST Invoices
Question: What are the mandatory particulars required on a valid GST tax invoice?
Keywords: gst invoice, tax invoice, mandatory invoice fields, hsn code, invoice rules, e-invoice, irn, qr code
Answer: Under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, a tax invoice issued by a registered taxpayer must contain specific mandatory elements: 1) Name, address, and GSTIN of the supplier; 2) A consecutive unique serial number (up to 16 characters); 3) Date of invoice issue; 4) Name, address, and GSTIN/UIN of the recipient if registered; 5) Harmonised System of Nomenclature (HSN) or SAC code of goods/services; 6) Description, quantity, unit, and total taxable value of the goods or services; 7) Tax rates applicable and the breakdown of CGST, SGST, IGST, and Cess; 8) Place of supply along with the name of the destination state; 9) Delivery address if different from place of supply; 10) Whether tax is payable on reverse charge; and 11) Signature or digital signature of the supplier. For businesses covered under mandatory e-invoicing thresholds, the Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and signed QR code generated via the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) are compulsory.
Source Name: Goods and Services Tax Portal
Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-009
Topic: E-way Bills
Question: When is an E-way bill required under GST and who is responsible for generating it?
Keywords: e-way bill, eway bill, ewb, goods movement, consignment value, 50000 threshold, transporter, eway bill portal
Answer: An Electronic Way Bill (E-way bill) is an electronic document generated on the e-way bill portal (ewaybillgst.gov.in) evidencing the movement of goods. Under Rule 138 of the CGST Rules, an e-way bill is mandatory for any movement of goods in a vehicle where the consignment value exceeds Rs 50,000 (whether for supply, inter-branch transfer, or inward supply from an unregistered person). Some states prescribe higher thresholds for purely intra-state movements. Part-A of the e-way bill captures GSTIN of supplier and recipient, place of delivery, invoice number, value, and HSN code; Part-B captures vehicle number and transporter details. The registered consignor, consignee, or the transporter is legally responsible for generating the e-way bill prior to commencement of transportation.
Source Name: National Informatics Centre (E-Way Bill System)
Source URL: https://ewaybillgst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: GST-010
Topic: GST Reconciliation
Question: What is GST reconciliation and why must taxpayers reconcile GSTR-2B with books of accounts?
Keywords: gst reconciliation, gstr-2b reconciliation, purchase register, itc mismatch, rule 37a, vendor follow up, audit trail
Answer: GST reconciliation is the systematic verification process of matching purchase invoices recorded in the company's internal books of accounts (Purchase Register) with the input tax details auto-populated in Form GSTR-2B on the GST portal. This exercise is critical because taxpayers can legally claim ITC only for invoices reflecting in GSTR-2B that were timely reported and paid by their suppliers. Reconciliation identifies missing vendor invoices, incorrect GSTINs, calculation discrepancies, duplicate ITC claims, and unfiled vendor returns. Regular monthly reconciliation protects the business against ITC reversals, punitive interest, notice issuances under Rule 37A or Section 73/74, and vendor non-compliance risks.
Source Name: Goods and Services Tax Portal
Source URL: https://www.gst.gov.in/

FAQ ID: TDS-001
Topic: TDS Fundamentals
Question: What is Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) and what is its objective?
Keywords: tds, tax deducted at source, income tax act, direct tax, withholding tax, advance tax collection, tax evasion prevention
Answer: Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a direct taxation mechanism under the Indian Income Tax Act, 1961, whereby the person or entity making specified payments (such as salary, professional fees, contractor payments, commission, rent, interest) is legally required to deduct a predetermined percentage of tax before disbursing the net payment to the recipient (payee). The deducted amount is remitted directly to the Central Government on behalf of the payee. The objective of TDS is to collect income tax at the source of income generation ("pay as you earn"), maintain a regular inflow of cash to the national exchequer, widen the tax base, and establish a digital audit trail to prevent tax evasion.
Source Name: Income Tax Department of India
Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/

Question: What types of payments are subject to TDS under the Income Tax Act?
Keywords: tds applicability, tds sections, section 192, section 194c, section 194j, section 194i, section 194h, section 194q, professional fees, contractor, rent
Answer: TDS applies to specific categories of payments made by individuals, firms, and companies exceeding prescribed statutory thresholds during a financial year. Major sections include: Section 192 (Salaries), Section 194A (Interest other than securities), Section 194C (Payments to contractors and sub-contractors), Section 194H (Commission or brokerage), Section 194I (Rent on land, buildings, plant, or machinery), Section 194J (Fees for professional or technical services and royalty), Section 194Q (TDS on purchase of goods exceeding Rs 50 Lakhs), and Section 195 (Payments to non-residents). The deductor must verify applicability, determine the appropriate section and rate, and ensure timely deduction.

FAQ ID: TDS-003
Topic: TDS Deductions and TAN
Question: What is a TAN and who is required to obtain it for TDS compliance?
Keywords: tan, tax deduction account number, 10 digit, mandatory tan, section 203a, deductor identification, nsdl, protean
Answer: TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number) is a 10-digit alphanumeric number allotted by the Income Tax Department under Section 203A of the Income Tax Act to persons responsible for deducting or collecting tax at source. The structure consists of 4 alphabetic characters (first three represent the jurisdiction code, the fourth is the initial letter of deductor's name), followed by 5 numeric digits and 1 alphabetic check digit (e.g., DELA12345B). Every deductor is legally required to quote their TAN in all TDS challans, TDS quarterly returns, and TDS certificates (Form 16/16A). Failure to apply for a TAN or failure to quote TAN attracts a statutory penalty of Rs 10,000 under Section 272BB.

FAQ ID: TDS-004
Topic: TDS Deposits
Question: How and when must deducted TDS be deposited with the Central Government?
Keywords: tds deposit, due date, 7th of next month, challan itns 281, bsr code, tin nsdl, late payment interest, section 201
Answer: TDS deducted during any calendar month must be deposited with the Central Government on or before the 7th day of the immediately following month (e.g., TDS deducted in October must be paid by November 7th). An exception exists for the month of March, where the payment deadline is extended to April 30th. Payments are made electronically through the Income Tax e-filing portal using Challan ITNS 281. Challans generate a Challan Identification Number (CIN) comprising the BSR code of the receiving bank branch, payment date, and challan serial number. Failure to deposit deducted tax attracts mandatory interest at 1.5% per month (or part of a month) under Section 201(1A) from the date of deduction to the date of actual deposit.
Source Name: Income Tax Department of India
Source URL: https://www.incometax.gov.in/

FAQ ID: TDS-005
Topic: TDS Returns
Question: What are quarterly TDS returns and what forms must be filed?
Keywords: tds returns, quarterly return, form 24q, form 26q, form 27q, form 27eq, tcs, section 200, late filing fee, section 234e
Answer: Every entity that has deducted TDS must file a quarterly TDS statement with the Income Tax Department under Section 200(3). The statutory forms are: Form 24Q for TDS on salary payments; Form 26Q for TDS on non-salary payments made to resident Indians (contractors, rent, professionals, interest); Form 27Q for payments made to non-residents; and Form 27EQ for Tax Collected at Source (TCS). The quarterly filing due dates are: Q1 (April-June) due by July 31; Q2 (July-September) due by October 31; Q3 (October-December) due by January 31; and Q4 (January-March) due by May 31. Late filing of TDS returns attracts a non-negotiable fee of Rs 200 per day under Section 234E until the return is submitted, capped at the total TDS amount.

Source URL: https://www.tdscpc.gov.in/

FAQ ID: TDS-006
Topic: Form 16
Question: What is Form 16 and when must an employer issue it to an employee?
Keywords: form 16, salary tds certificate, part a, part b, employer, income from salary, section 203, traces, june 15
Answer: Form 16 is an annual certificate of tax deduction issued under Section 203 of the Income Tax Act by an employer to an employee whose income from salary was subjected to TDS under Section 192. It comprises two parts: Part A is generated and downloaded from the government TRACES portal, containing deductor and employee details, TAN, PAN, and quarterly TDS deposit summaries with challan CINs; Part B is prepared by the employer, containing a detailed breakdown of gross salary, exempt allowances under Section 10, deductions under Chapter VI-A (Sections 80C, 80D, etc.), and net tax calculation. Form 16 must be issued to the employee annually on or before June 15 following the financial year.
Source Name: TRACES Portal (Income Tax Department)

Topic: Form 16A
Question: What is Form 16A and how does it differ from Form 16?
Keywords: form 16a, non salary tds certificate, quarterly certificate, professional fees, contract payment, interest, traces
Answer: Form 16A is a quarterly TDS certificate issued under Section 203 for tax deductions made on payments other than salary (covered under sections such as 194A, 194C, 194H, 194I, 194J, and 194Q). FAQ ID: TDS-008
Topic: Form 26AS
Question: What is Form 26AS and how is it used to verify tax credits?
Keywords: form 26as, annual tax credit statement, traces, e-filing portal, tax paid, tds credit, advance tax, self assessment tax
Answer: Form 26AS is an annual consolidated tax credit statement linked to a taxpayer's PAN, viewable and downloadable via the Income Tax e-filing portal or TRACES. It reflects: 1) Details of tax deducted at source (TDS) by various payers on behalf of the taxpayer; 2) Details of tax collected at source (TCS); 3) Advance tax and self-assessment tax paid directly by the taxpayer; 4) Details of income tax refunds received during the financial year; and 5) High-value financial transactions reported under the Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT). Taxpayers use Form 26AS to cross-check that all taxes deducted by their employers and clients have been successfully credited to their PAN before filing their Income Tax Return (ITR).

Question: What is the Annual Information Statement (AIS) and how does it relate to TDS?
Keywords: ais, tis, annual information statement, tax information summary, comprehensive financial reporting, sft, dividends, feedback
Answer: The Annual Information Statement (AIS) is a comprehensive financial information ledger introduced by the Income Tax Department that provides an expanded view beyond Form 26AS. FAQ ID: TDS-010
Topic: TDS Mismatches
Question: How should TDS mismatches between Form 26AS/AIS and financial records be resolved?
Keywords: tds mismatch, 26as mismatch, rectification, correction return, pan error, deductor rectification, traces correction
Answer: A TDS mismatch occurs when tax deducted from a payee's income is not reflected or is reflected with a lower amount in their Form 26AS or AIS. This typically happens due to: the deductor failing to file their TDS return; quoting an incorrect PAN; depositing tax under an erroneous TAN or challan CIN; or entering incorrect payment amounts. To resolve a mismatch: 1) The payee should verify deduction certificates (Form 16/16A) against bank statements; 2) The payee must notify the deductor and request them to verify their filed Form 24Q/26Q; 3) The deductor must file an online TDS Correction Return on TRACES to rectify the PAN, challan, or amount error; and 4) Once processed by TRACES, the updated credit reflects in the payee's Form 26AS, allowing them to claim full credit without tax demand notices.
Source Name: TRACES Portal (Income Tax Department)
Source URL: https://www.tdscpc.gov.in/

FAQ ID: MCA-003
Topic: ROC Annual Filings
Question: What are ROC annual statutory filings (Form AOC-4 and Form MGT-7)?
Keywords: roc annual filing, aoc-4, mgt-7, mgt-7a, financial statements, annual return, balance sheet, agm, 30 days, 60 days
Answer: Every company registered in India must complete annual statutory filings with the ROC via the MCA portal following the conclusion of its Annual General Meeting (AGM). The two primary forms are: 1) Form AOC-4 (for filing financial statements, Balance Sheet, Profit and Loss Account, Directors' Report, and Auditor's Report), due within 30 days of the AGM; and 2) Form MGT-7 or MGT-7A (for filing the company's Annual Return covering shareholding patterns, indebtedness, directors, and board meetings), due within 60 days of the AGM. For companies holding AGMs by September 30, AOC-4 is due by October 30 and MGT-7 by November 29. Late filing attracts an additional fee of Rs 100 per day per form under Section 403, and persistent default risks director disqualification.

FAQ ID: MCA-005
Topic: CIN
Question: What is Corporate Identity Number (CIN) and how is it structured?
Keywords: cin, corporate identity number, 21 digit, listed unlisted, state code, incorporation year, company classification
Answer: The Corporate Identity Number (CIN) is a unique 21-digit alphanumeric identity code assigned by the ROC to every company incorporated in India. The 21 characters encode specific legal attributes: Character 1 indicates listing status ('L' for listed, 'U' for unlisted); characters 2 to 6 represent the 5-digit industry classification Economic Activity Code (based on National Industrial Classification - NIC); characters 7 and 8 represent the 2-letter state code of the registered office (e.g., 'MH' for Maharashtra, 'DL' for Delhi); characters 9 to 12 represent the 4-digit year of company incorporation (e.g., '2023'); characters 13 to 15 represent the company ownership classification (e.g., 'PTC' for Private Limited, 'PLC' for Public Limited, 'GOI' for Government of India); and the final 6 digits (16 to 21) are the sequential registration number allotted by the ROC. The CIN must be quoted on all corporate letterheads, invoices, notices, and publications under Section 12(3)(c).

Answer: A Director Identification Number (DIN) is a unique 8-digit identification number allotted by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs under Section 154 of the Companies Act to any individual intending to be appointed as a director in an Indian company. FAQ ID: MCA-007
Topic: Director Compliance
Question: What are director compliance obligations and grounds for disqualification under Section 164?
Keywords: director compliance, disqualification, section 164(2), 3 years non filing, director duties, section 166, board meetings
Answer: Directors owe statutory duties under Section 166 of the Companies Act, 2013, including acting in good faith, exercising reasonable care, avoiding conflicts of interest, and ensuring corporate statutory compliance. Under Section 164(2), a director becomes automatically disqualified from being reappointed in that company or appointed to any other company for a period of 5 years if the company: 1) Has not filed financial statements or annual returns for any continuous period of 3 financial years; or 2) Has failed to repay deposits, pay interest, redeem debentures, or pay declared dividends for one year or more. Disqualification leads to automatic vacation of directorship in all other companies under Section 167(1)(a). Directors must also disclose directorships and interests annually in Form MBP-1 and confirm eligibility in Form DIR-8.
